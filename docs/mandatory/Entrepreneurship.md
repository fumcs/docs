**Course syllabus:**

- Introduction to entrepreneurial thinking and the role of entrepreneurship in the digital and knowledge-based economy; characteristics and competencies of successful entrepreneurs; opportunity recognition and idea generation in technology-driven and service-oriented contexts related to computer science and information technology.

- Familiarity with startup ecosystems, with emphasis on regional examples (such as data science, digital economy, and smart systems ventures originating from universities); guest talks and/or site visits to local startups to illustrate practical challenges and success factors.

- Case studies of major successes and failures in launching innovative ventures, including film documentaries or dramatizations related to startup founding; discussion of lessons learned about timing, team composition, product–market fit, and scaling.

- Basic strategic questions for startups (for example, key strategic questions frameworks, “jobs-to-be-done” perspective, and similar approaches); using these tools to refine value propositions and business ideas.

- Introduction to business models, particularly the Lean Startup and Business Model Canvas perspectives; high-level overview of revenue models, cost structures, and key partners and resources.

- Introduction to value proposition design and customer development; designing experiments to test assumptions about customers and value; using structured customer interviews (e.g., “Mom Test”-style techniques) to validate or refute hypotheses; understanding pivots in business models.

- Fundamentals of team building and teamwork skills in entrepreneurial settings; collaboration, communication, and conflict management in startup teams.

- Overview of incubators, accelerators, and innovation parks; basic requirements and processes for admission to early-stage growth programs and idea-focused events.

- Basics of pitching: preparing and delivering short presentations of ideas to potential investors, partners, or competition juries; structure of a pitch deck and common pitfalls in presenting technical ideas to non-technical audiences.
