**Course syllabus:**

This course provides an introduction to fundamental concepts of combinatorics with an emphasis on techniques widely used in computer science. The focus is on counting methods, discrete structures, and rigorous combinatorial reasoning.

Topics include: rapid review of sets, functions, algorithms, and propositional logic (including Boolean algebra); basic counting principles (sum and product rules); pigeonhole principle; permutations and combinations; binomial coefficients and the binomial theorem; inclusion–exclusion principle and its applications; recurrence relations and methods for solving them; and generating functions.

Relations and their types: representation of relations; equivalence relations and partitions; partial orders and total orders; closure of relations with respect to different properties; and connections to topics covered in “Foundations of Mathematical Sciences,” in coordination to avoid duplication.

Matrices from a combinatorial perspective: properties of 0–1 matrices relevant to combinatorics; introduction to Hadamard matrices and some of their properties; preparation for studying Latin squares and graphs via matrix representations.

Graphs and graph-based models: definition of graphs with emphasis on modeling applications; basic graph-theoretic notions such as paths, cycles, degree, degree sequences, and connectivity; main graph classes including complete graphs, trees, bipartite graphs, Eulerian and Hamiltonian graphs, directed graphs, and tournaments; perfect and maximum matchings (including algorithmic ideas and applications); graph coloring and chromatic polynomials, illustrated with examples and algorithms.

Latin squares, designs, and finite geometries: introduction to definitions and basic concepts; emphasis on relationships among these objects and with previously introduced concepts such as graphs; examples and applications; systems of distinct representatives and Hall’s marriage theorem, illustrated with examples and applications to Latin squares and other combinatorial structures.
