**Course syllabus:**

The course focuses on optimization over discrete structures. After reviewing basic notions of optimization and introducing the concept of discrete optimization, the course emphasizes modeling discrete problems and designing exact and approximate solution methods. Topics include formulating problems as discrete optimization models; modeling with binary and integer variables; local search methods and neighborhood structures; constructive, improvement, and hybrid heuristics; metaheuristics such as simulated annealing, tabu search, and genetic algorithms; and fundamental concepts of network optimization. Classical combinatorial optimization problems (such as shortest path, minimum spanning tree, maximum flow, matching, knapsack, traveling salesperson, and facility location) are considered as case studies, and various algorithmic approaches (exact, heuristic, and metaheuristic) are presented for them. The course also introduces complexity aspects and the distinction between polynomially solvable and NP-hard problems, together with the role of approximation algorithms.
