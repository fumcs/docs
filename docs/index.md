## A) Introduction

Over recent decades the use of computers and digital services has become a pervasive and unavoidable feature of modern life.  
The expansion of online businesses (for example e-commerce platforms and ride-hailing services), the growth of social networks, and the gradual transformation of traditional industries into services built on computer networks all illustrate the central role of computing in contemporary society.

Computer Science is not merely the study of computers as physical devices.  
In a broad and widely accepted sense it may be described as the study of algorithms, information, and computation, together with their realisation in hardware and software systems.  
From this point of view Computer Science is fundamentally concerned with formulating problems and designing, analysing, and implementing algorithmic solutions to them.

Important subfields of Computer Science include, among others:

1. **Theory of computation** – automata theory, computability, computational complexity, formal languages, logic, and related topics.
2. **Data structures and algorithms** – algorithm analysis and design, data structures, combinatorial optimisation, and related techniques.
3. **Programming languages and methodologies** – programming language theory, type systems, program analysis and verification, software engineering methods, and programming paradigms.
4. **Systems** – operating systems, databases, computer networks, distributed systems, web systems, and systems architecture.
5. **Computer architecture and hardware** – digital logic, computer organisation, microarchitecture, and hardware–software interaction.
6. **Artificial intelligence and learning** – artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning, data mining, natural language processing, and recommender systems.
7. **Data-oriented subfields** – data science, computational statistics, and related methods for analysing and interpreting data.
8. **Quantum and unconventional computation** – quantum algorithms and models, as well as other non-classical models of computation.
9. **Scientific computing** – numerical methods and high-performance computing for the simulation and analysis of scientific and engineering problems.
10. **Mathematical modelling and applied methods** – differential equations, optimisation, stochastic models, and related tools used in engineering and the sciences.
11. **Numerical and computational methods** – numerical linear algebra, numerical solution of differential equations, and algorithms for continuous problems.
12. **Modelling of complex systems** – modelling, simulation, and analysis of complex, networked, and multi-agent systems.
13. **Computer graphics and visualisation** – 2D and 3D graphics, visualisation, and human–computer interaction.
14. **Mathematical foundations** – discrete mathematics, combinatorics, graph theory, probability theory, and other supporting topics.
15. **Interdisciplinary applications** – connections with fields such as cognitive science, bioinformatics, computational economics, and many others.

In the past two decades *data science* has emerged as an important field at the intersection of statistics, probability, computer science, and several empirical disciplines.  
In many universities this area is considered a specialisation or close relative of Computer Science.  
Programs in data science typically place simultaneous emphasis on probability, statistics, and computation.  
The present curriculum has been designed with this wider context in mind so as to

- avoid unnecessary duplication of material between Computer Science and closely related data-oriented programs, and  
- allow data-science-style topics to be covered, where appropriate, within existing course titles.

## B) Mission of the Department and Programme

The overarching mission of the undergraduate programme is to educate specialists who are well prepared in mathematics, statistics, and computer science, and who can contribute to the development of science and technology at the local, national, and regional levels.  
On the one hand the programme emphasises solid theoretical foundations; on the other it stresses practical skills and familiarity with modern tools and environments.

More specifically, the programme aims to:

- provide students with a rigorous grounding in the mathematical, statistical, and computational principles that underpin modern computer science;
- develop the ability to model and analyse real-world problems and to design effective algorithmic and software solutions for them;
- prepare graduates for postgraduate study in Computer Science and related disciplines, as well as for professional work in software development, data-driven industries, and research and development;
- foster teamwork, clear written and oral communication, and awareness of ethical and professional issues in the design and deployment of computing technologies.

## C) Educational Goals

The educational goals of the undergraduate Computer Science programme can be grouped as follows.

1. **Scientific literacy** – Graduates should possess the mathematical and computational background needed to read, understand, and apply scientific texts and research in computer science and related fields.
2. **Professional competence** – Graduates should be able to design, implement, test, and maintain software and computational systems that meet specified requirements, using appropriate methods and tools.
3. **Research and innovation** – Graduates should be capable of participating in research and development projects, identifying open problems, and contributing to the creation of new methods, algorithms, and applications.

These goals are supported by the basic, mandatory, and elective courses, as well as by project-based and practical components embedded in the curriculum.

## D) Relationship with Other Fields

Computer Science interacts naturally with mathematics, statistics, electrical engineering, computer engineering, economics, cognitive science, and many other disciplines.  
This curriculum has been designed so that:

- students can meaningfully collaborate with experts from other fields on interdisciplinary projects (for example in scientific computing, data analysis, or computational modelling); and
- graduates have the background needed to pursue advanced study in related programmes such as data science, artificial intelligence, computational statistics, and applied mathematics.

## E) Graduate Profile

Graduates of this programme are expected to:

1. apply mathematical, statistical, and computational tools to analyse and solve real-world problems;
2. formulate problems precisely, design appropriate algorithms and data structures, and reason about their correctness and efficiency;
3. design and develop software systems using modern programming languages, libraries, and development environments, following sound engineering practices;
4. understand fundamental notions such as computability, decidability, and complexity, and recognise the inherent limitations of algorithmic solutions;
5. work effectively with operating systems, computer networks, and database systems, and utilise them when building applications;
6. analyse data using statistical methods, machine-learning techniques, and data-mining tools, and interpret the results critically;
7. communicate technical ideas clearly, both orally and in writing, and work productively in teams; and
8. engage in lifelong learning and stay current with emerging technologies, methods, and tools.

## F) Summary

The B.Sc. curriculum in Computer Science at Ferdowsi University of Mashhad is intended to address both current needs and future trends in information and communication technologies.  
It combines strong theoretical foundations with sustained attention to practical skills, considers emerging areas such as data science and machine learning, and offers a broad range of electives.  
Together, these features provide students with the flexibility to shape their own educational paths while maintaining a robust core in Computer Science.
