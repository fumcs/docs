**Course syllabus:**

Scatter plots. Simple linear regression. Least-squares estimators; maximum-likelihood estimators and their properties. Underlying assumptions of the linear regression model. Hypothesis tests and confidence intervals for model parameters. Prediction intervals for single observations and for the mean response. Simple regression through the origin; coefficient of determination.

Multiple linear regression: expressing the multiple linear regression model in matrix notation; least-squares and maximum-likelihood estimation of the parameters; properties of estimators; estimation and interpretation of standardized regression coefficients; analysis-of-variance (ANOVA) table; significance tests for regression coefficients; residual analysis and checking underlying model assumptions in multiple linear regression; coefficient of determination and related measures. Partial correlations. Variable-selection methods for explanatory variables in multiple linear regression (enter, forward, backward, and stepwise procedures). Introduction to confirmatory approaches to model building. Model-selection criteria (Cp, Mallows, PRESS, AIC). Transformations for converting certain nonlinear regression models (in terms of the variables) to linear regression models.

**Teaching and learning strategies (aligned with content and objectives):**

Lectures and instructor-led presentations; solving illustrative examples and related exercises in class; active student participation in discussions through problem solving and short quizzes. The course includes a project in which students analyze real data sets. In addition to theoretical instruction, the course emphasizes developing data-analysis skills and proficiency with statistical software, which should be supported through hands-on sessions in a computer laboratory.

Required facilities: regular classroom plus a well-equipped computer laboratory.
