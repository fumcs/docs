**Course syllabus:**

This course introduces the basic concepts and analytical tools of non-cooperative game theory, with an emphasis on applications to economics, computer science, and the social sciences.

Topics include: strategic-form games; dominance and best-response analysis; Nash equilibrium in pure and mixed strategies; extensive-form games with perfect and imperfect information; subgame perfection; repeated games; Bayesian games and games of incomplete information; mechanism design and implementation; auctions and bidding strategies; and selected topics in algorithmic game theory, such as congestion games and cost-sharing.

Students learn to model strategic interactions formally, to reason about rational behavior in multi-agent settings, and to apply game-theoretic reasoning to real-world problems and engineering scenarios.
