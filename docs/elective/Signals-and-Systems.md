**Course syllabus:**

- Fundamental concepts; continuous-time and discrete-time signals; independent variables and their transformations; classification of signals and systems; elementary (basis) signals in continuous and discrete time; system properties including memory, invertibility, causality, stability, linearity, and time invariance.

- Linear time-invariant (LTI) systems; continuous-time and discrete-time convolution; efficient methods for discrete convolution; properties of convolution; impulse response and properties of LTI systems; block-diagram representations of LTI systems.

- Continuous-time Fourier series; generalized Fourier series and orthogonal functions; orthogonal functions of Legendre, Walsh, Laguerre, and complex exponentials; response of LTI systems to complex exponential signals; properties and convergence of Fourier series.

- Continuous-time Fourier transform; properties of the Fourier transform; block-diagram representation of LTI systems using the Fourier transform; frequency response and solution of differential equations for LTI systems; Fourier transforms of important elementary functions.

- Applications of the continuous-time Fourier transform: filter types; sampling; modulation and demodulation; multiple-access techniques.

- Discrete-time Fourier series; differences from the continuous-time Fourier series; response of LTI systems using the discrete-time Fourier series; properties of the discrete-time Fourier series.

- Discrete-time Fourier transform (DTFT); properties of the DTFT; digital filters and the DTFT; frequency response and solution of difference equations for discrete-time systems; block-diagram representation of discrete-time systems using the DTFT; discrete Fourier transform (DFT).

- Z-transform and its properties.

**References:**

- Oppenheim, A. V., Willsky, A. S., & Nawab, S. H. (1997). Signals and Systems (2nd ed.). Prentice Hall.
