**Course syllabus:**

The aim of this course is to introduce students to the process of constructing, analyzing, and interpreting mathematical models of real-world phenomena, with a focus on problems arising in science, engineering, and data-driven contexts.

Topics include: the modeling process; identifying assumptions, variables, and parameters; dimensional analysis and nondimensionalization; simple deterministic models using algebraic and differential equations; discrete-time and continuous-time models; linear and nonlinear models; basic stability and sensitivity analysis.

Applications may be drawn from population dynamics, epidemic models, traffic flow, queuing systems, resource management, finance, and other areas, at the instructor’s discretion. Emphasis is placed on translating verbal descriptions into mathematical models, solving or simulating the resulting systems, and interpreting results in the original context.

Students are expected to engage in project-based work, including formulation, analysis, and presentation of one or more models for selected problems, possibly using computational tools such as Python, MATLAB, or similar environments.
