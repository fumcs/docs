**Course syllabus:**

This course introduces the fundamental models and questions of the theory of computation. The focus is on formalizing what it means for a function or language to be computable, and on classifying computational problems according to their complexity.

Topics include: finite automata and regular languages; deterministic and nondeterministic finite automata; regular expressions; closure and decision properties of regular languages; pumping lemma for regular languages. Context-free grammars and pushdown automata; normal forms; basic parsing ideas; pumping lemma for context-free languages; examples and limitations of context-free languages.

Turing machines and general models of computation: variants of Turing machines and their equivalence; Church–Turing thesis; decidability; examples of decidable and undecidable problems; reductions; the halting problem and related classical results. Introduction to complexity theory: time and space complexity; classes P and NP; NP-completeness via polynomial-time reductions; selected NP-complete problems and their significance.
