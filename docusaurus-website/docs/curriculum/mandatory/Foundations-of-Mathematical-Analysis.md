**Course syllabus:**

This course develops the foundations of real analysis, with an emphasis on metric spaces and topological concepts that underlie modern analysis and many areas of theoretical computer science. It builds on basic real analysis and introduces more abstract settings.

Topics include: the real numbers and metric spaces; examples of metric spaces and their basic topological notions; open and closed sets, interior, closure, and boundary; convergence of sequences in metric spaces; Cauchy sequences and completeness; compactness and Heine–Borel-type characterizations; connectedness; continuous functions between metric spaces; uniform continuity; preservation of compactness and connectedness by continuous maps; and further topics at the instructor’s discretion (such as contraction mappings and fixed-point theorems).

The focus is on understanding definitions and proofs, with many examples and counterexamples to illustrate the general theory.
