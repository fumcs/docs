**Course syllabus:**

This course introduces the logical formalisms and reasoning techniques that underlie much of theoretical computer science and formal methods.

Topics include: propositional logic (syntax, semantics, satisfiability, and validity); normal forms and resolution; first-order predicate logic; structures and interpretations; proof systems and natural deduction; soundness and completeness (at an introductory level); decidability and complexity issues in propositional and first-order logic; automated theorem proving and satisfiability solvers (SAT/SMT); and basics of modal and temporal logics.

The course also covers logics for program specification and verification, such as Hoare logic and simple temporal logics, and illustrates their use in reasoning about programs and systems.

**References:**

- Ben-Ari, M. (2012). Mathematical Logic for Computer Science (3rd ed.). Springer.

- Enderton, H. B. (2001). A Mathematical Introduction to Logic (2nd ed.). Academic Press.
