**Course syllabus:**

Cartesian coordinates, polar coordinates, complex numbers and their addition, multiplication, and roots; different representations of complex numbers; numerical sequences; limits and related theorems; infinite limits and limits at infinity; one-sided limits; continuity and sequential continuity; the intermediate value theorem for continuous functions; the extreme value theorem for continuous functions; derivative and differentiation rules; inverse functions and their derivatives; derivatives of trigonometric functions and of their inverses; the chain rule; Fermat’s theorem (vanishing of the derivative at a point of extremum); Rolle’s theorem; the mean value theorem; Cauchy’s mean value theorem; first- and second-derivative tests for extrema; concavity of curves; inflection points; differential of a function; Taylor expansion; geometric and physical applications of the derivative; curves; velocity and acceleration in polar coordinates; applications of the derivative in approximating roots of equations; definition of the integral of functions and integrability; mean value theorem for integrals; fundamental theorems of differential and integral calculus; antiderivatives; approximate methods for evaluating integrals; applications of integration in computing area, volume, and arc length; logarithm and exponential function and their derivatives; hyperbolic functions; techniques of integration (all methods); introduction to the Gamma function and its properties; sequences and series viewed as functions; numerical series; convergence theorems such as the ratio test, root test, and others; Stirling’s approximation; convergence theorems for power series and Taylor’s theorem with and without remainder. 

**Teaching and learning strategies appropriate to the content and objectives:**

Since an elementary familiarity with the concepts of integration is necessary for the course Probability and Statistics I, it is advisable to arrange the course so that students become acquainted with the concept of the integral early in the semester.
